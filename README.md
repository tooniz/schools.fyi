# schools.fyi

A levels.fyi-style leveling comparison for Ontario schools. The home page lines up independent schools and external curricula against Ontario across three separate findings: **when content appears, how deeply it is treated, and how quickly the sequence moves**. An accelerated Grade 5 can appear against Ontario Grade 6 without forcing every enriched course to pretend it is a later grade.

## How the leveling works

Ontario is the reference axis: each Ontario grade occupies exactly one progress row, JK through Grade 12, plus a small region for work that sits past Grade 12. Every other program is described by `OffsetRule`s in `data/leveling/`, one per program, subject, and grade band:

- **`offsetYears`** is content timing. It shifts a level along the axis; `+0.5` means comparable material appears half a grade earlier.
- **`spanYears`** stretches a level across more than one Ontario row. This is how curriculum compression is shown: TFS covers Ontario Grades 6–8 in two years, so those two levels are drawn 1.5 rows tall.
- **`depth`** is `on-standard`, `enriched`, `advanced`, or `not-assessed`. A positive timing offset never raises it automatically.
- **`pace`** is `on-standard`, `faster`, `variable`, or `not-assessed`. It is likewise independent of timing.
- **`coverage`** marks bands a program does not teach, which is how Ontario's missing core French before Grade 4 is represented rather than faked.
- **`confidence`** is one of `documented`, `approximate`, `community-reported`, or `insufficient-evidence`. A zero offset backed by an explicit school statement ("depth rather than breadth") is *documented*, not *insufficient evidence*; the two are kept distinct.
- **`Program.pathways`** defines required, optional, reach-ahead, or placement-based routes. Sparse rules carrying **`pathwayId`** replace only the published grade bands for that route, so a user can switch one school column without duplicating the school or averaging a minority track into the cohort.
- **`acceleratedPathway`** remains as sourced prose on older placements while those routes are migrated to structured pathway rules.
- **`evidenceIds`** must resolve, and official/school sources are displayed separately from community discussion.

The validator enforces that every taught grade of every program has exactly one default rule per subject, alternative rules reference a declared pathway, bands do not double-cover within one pathway, and no selectable route runs backwards along the axis. Overlap *is* allowed in the data, because convergence is real.

### Drawing the matrix

`layoutMatrix` turns those rules into boxes, and it makes two adjustments so the picture stays readable without moving any level off the position its offset claims:

- **Gaps after taught levels are closed by stretching backwards.** When a level jumps ahead of another taught level, it is drawn down to meet its predecessor instead of leaving a hole — Bayview Glen's SK is drawn across Ontario SK *and* Grade 1. A pathway beginning after explicit `not-offered` years is the exception: its lead-in remains blank so Immersion Grade 1 can visibly begin at the Core Grade 4 position.
- **Crowding widens the row for everyone.** Where a program puts two levels on the same stretch of the axis, that stretch is given proportionally more height and every other column crossing it grows to match, so converging levels stack instead of overlapping. Bayview Glen's Grade 8 and Grade 9 both sit at Ontario Grade 9 in mathematics, so that row is drawn double height in every column.

Both are pure layout: `progressStart` and `progressEnd` keep the unadjusted values, and the tests assert that taught ladders do not overlap and only the intentional post-absence lead-in can remain blank.

Three smaller rules keep empty space from reading as missing data. Years outside a program's range are labelled rather than left blank — "UTS starts at Grade 7", "RHMS ends after Grade 8" — and the spare rows past Grade 12 are only drawn when some level actually reaches them, so an all-on-pace comparison does not trail off into a dead band. A program with no rules at all for the selected subject keeps its column and says *not researched yet*, because silently dropping it would read as "this school does not exist" rather than "we have not done this work". Column headers also distinguish a zero we can stand behind from one we cannot measure: *level with Ontario* means the placement is evidenced, *no documented offset* means it is not.

## Pages

- `/` — the matrix. Subject and per-column pathway controls, up to five programs at once, separate timing/depth/pace details, and an optional non-ranked post-secondary outcomes panel.
- `/questions` — every placement below `documented`, derived from the dataset rather than maintained by hand, each with the specific document that would settle it and a prefilled report link. A placement leaves this page the moment a real source arrives.
- `/sources` — all evidence records grouped by kind, with publisher, quote, access date, and which programs cite them.
- `/program/[id]` — one static page per program: methodology, grade range, every per-subject placement, and its sources.
- `/contribute` — the report form, prefillable from anywhere else on the site.

## Development and checks

Requires Node 20.9+. Run `npm install`, then `npm run dev`. Quality gates are `npm run validate:data`, `npm run typecheck`, `npm run lint`, `npm test`, and `npm run build`.

## Data workflow

Leveling data lives under `data/leveling/`: `programs.ts` (columns and their own level names), the school/curriculum modules, the evidence records (every citable source, with the quote that supports the placement), and the offset rules. `outcomes.ts` keeps post-secondary disclosures in their original units and carries explicit quality flags. Expectation-level detail for the older strand-by-strand comparison lives under `data/<provider>/<subject>.ts`.

Each program carries three names, because they are read in different amounts of space: `name` is the formal one used for search, `displayName` heads the column and the picker, and `abbreviation` labels the selected-track chip. Programs are tinted by `kind` — curriculum, school, framework, or enrichment program — so the type of thing being compared is visible before reading a word. `kind` answers *what a program is*; the optional `framework: "ib" | "ap"` answers *whose curriculum a school teaches*, which is a separate question, since Branksome Hall and Upper Canada College are independent schools **and** IB schools at once. A third field, `frameworkScope`, records how far the framework reaches, and it exists because the framework's name does not tell you: `curriculum` means the school runs it grade after grade as its own programme, while `courses` means an Ontario school whose students opt into individual courses in the senior years. Every AP school on this site is the second kind — Havergal's own page says it offers "timetabled Advanced Placement (AP) courses in some subjects" — so collapsing the two would make three senior electives look like an IB continuum.

A school keeps its own colour whichever framework it teaches, and the badge carries the framework instead: filled for `curriculum`, outlined for `courses`, with a tooltip that spells the difference out. Colour is left to answer one question — what kind of thing is this column — because two frameworks share one hue and it cannot answer both. Validation rejects the tag on anything that is not a school and rejects a framework stated without its scope; both are set only where a first-party source states them, so silence stays silent. Single-sex schools carry `admits: "boys" | "girls"`, rendered as ♂ or ♀ beside the display name; the glyph is a separate element with a visually-hidden label, because the character alone is announced as "male sign" and because `displayName` also feeds search and the report form's select.

Source freshness is checked, not just recorded. `npm run validate:data` warns about any source not re-read within twelve months, the interval `CONTRIBUTING.md` promises, and the same sources are flagged *needs re-check* in the UI. It warns rather than fails: a stale citation may still be accurate, but it is a placement nobody has defended lately.

To add or change a placement: quote a primary source, record its URL and access date, write the `claim` and `rationale` so a reader can disagree with your reasoning, and pick the confidence tier honestly. Follow `CONTRIBUTING.md` and run all gates.

## Deployment

Pushes to `main` deploy a static export to the default GitHub Pages project URL. In the repository's **Settings → Pages**, select **GitHub Actions** as the source. The deployment workflow supplies GitHub's project base path, builds the site, and publishes `out/`.

To reproduce the Pages build locally, run `STATIC_EXPORT=true NEXT_PUBLIC_BASE_PATH=/schools.fyi npm run build` and serve `out/`.

### Wiring up the report form (one-time)

There is no backend — the deploy is a static export — so reports go to a hosted form service. The POST is deliberately vendor-neutral (`FormData` with `Accept: application/json`), which every one of Formspree, Basin, Getform, and Static Forms accepts, so switching providers is a one-line change.

1. Create a form at whichever service you prefer and copy its endpoint URL. Note the free-tier limits differ a lot: Formspree allows 50 submissions per month account-wide, Static Forms advertises 500.
2. In **Settings → Secrets and variables → Actions → Variables**, add a repository *variable* named `NEXT_PUBLIC_REPORT_ENDPOINT` with that URL.

Use a variable, not a secret. Anything prefixed `NEXT_PUBLIC_` is string-replaced into the client bundle at build time and is therefore public by design; storing it as a secret would hide it from you without hiding it from anyone else.

Leaving it unset is safe and is the default. The form then submits to a prefilled issue on this repository instead of showing a dead button, and says so on the page. For local development, put the endpoint in `.env.local`.

## Scope and limitations

The columns are the Ontario and Singapore curricula, thirteen GTA independent schools (the previous twelve plus The York School), two international frameworks (Advanced Placement and International Baccalaureate), and two enrichment programs (Kumon and Spirit of Math), across mathematics, English/language, French, and science. Five can be compared at once; adding a sixth drops the leftmost. Ontario's French column has selectable Core, Extended, and Immersion pathways rather than three duplicate Ontario programs.

The default schools are Bayview Glen, Upper Canada College, Havergal College, and Branksome Hall alongside Ontario. Bayview Glen is fixed by product choice; the other three were the highest-interest school topics in Ontario Google Trends Web Search for the trailing year ending September 2, 2026. The measurement uses Bayview Glen as a common anchor across five-term batches; Branksome and Appleby were within roughly two per cent and can reverse under Trends sampling.

Science is the newest subject and is deliberately incomplete. The validator only enforces full grade coverage for a program/subject pair that already has at least one rule, so science can land school by school as the sourcing is done rather than all at once. A program with no science rules renders as an explained empty column — "not researched yet", with a link to contribute — rather than vanishing from the comparison.

What the current data does and does not support:

- **Bayview Glen** is the clearest whole-cohort acceleration. Its Director of Teaching and Learning states the mapping outright — "Our Senior Kindergarten students do the Grade 1 math curriculum, and our Grade 1s do the Grade 2 curriculum–and so on" — the Prep School page claims students work "one year ahead of their peers in other schools", and the Grade 6 booklet prints its mathematics section under a "Grade 7 Curriculum" heading. Mathematics is `+1.0` and `documented` from SK to Grade 8.
- **TFS** is the most structurally different: the school states that "our students have earned most Ontario Grade 9, 10 and 11 credits a year ahead of their peers in the province", and it compresses Ontario Grades 6–8 into two years. It is also the only program that sits *behind* Ontario anywhere — there is no English instruction at all before Grade 2, because "from Jardin d'éveil Hafez (age 2) to Grade 1, our program is entirely in French".
- **Havergal** is placed level with Ontario in mathematics and English *by its own stated design* — it teaches "for depth rather than for breadth" and is openly sceptical of condensed programs. French is the exception and is `+1.0` for every student from Grade 3 through Grade 9: the course calendar has Grade 7 taking Grade 8 French and Grade 8 taking FSF1D, the Ontario Grade 9 course, "for all students".
- **BSS** keeps the Ontario sequence, and its Junior School page is specific enough to check: the number range taught in each grade (to 50 in Grade 1, to 10,000 in Grade 4, to 1,000,000 in Grade 6) matches Ontario exactly, grade for grade. Its one documented acceleration is an opt-in Grade 7–8 math and French stream, which 25% of Grade 8 students use to earn a Grade 9 credit. That minority track is recorded as a pathway rather than blended into the cohort's level.
- **UTS is the one placement that can be checked course code by course code.** Its own course chart shows Ontario codes taken a year early throughout: MPM1D in Grade 8, MPM2D in Grade 9, MCR3U in Grade 10, MHF4U in Grade 11. The Grade 7 year is a UTS-internal course with no Ontario code, so it is drawn two rows tall — absorbing Ontario Grades 7 and 8 is the only way the following year reaches Grade 9 math. The lead is *not* spent finishing early: Grade 12 returns to Ontario Grade 12 courses, so the last two years converge on the same row and the year gained buys AP breadth instead. English is the instructive contrast — the same chart shows ENG1D through ENG4U dead on the Ontario grade, and UTS advertises acceleration for mathematics and science on the same page while never once claiming it for English. Two cautions: the course chart is the 2015–16 calendar, corroborated only qualitatively by the current site, and UTS accelerates the whole cohort while explicitly refusing individual acceleration — "Individual fast tracking and early course specialization are not compatible with our philosophy." Science sits between the two: the school claims acceleration "throughout the Foundation and Middle years" without quantifying it, so Grades 7–8 take the half-grade floor, while the calendar's placement of SBI 3UE — Ontario Grade 11 Biology — in "Middle IV or Senior V" with prerequisite SNC 2D supports a full year for Grades 9–10, since that schedule requires Grade 10 science to be finished by Grade 9.
- **RHMS is the counterexample, and it is here on purpose.** It is a private school that sits level with Ontario on its own evidence rather than for want of evidence. Arguing *against* multi-age classes, RHMS uses mathematics as its example — "in a single Grade three class, the teacher would teach Grade three math concepts" — and concludes that single grades ensure "the appropriate curriculum, at the appropriate time." Its mathematics page claims rigour and never claims a grade advantage. French begins in Grade 4, exactly when Ontario's does, which makes it the only column that starts French on the same row as the province. Two structural facts worth knowing: the Montessori programme covers only the Kindergarten years, so Grades 1–8 are conventional single-grade classes and no Lower/Upper Elementary triads exist here; and there is no secondary school, so Grades 9–12 are empty. Its CAT4 averages are not used — an achievement score cannot be converted into a curriculum offset.
- **AP and IB are overlays, not schools, and are scoped accordingly.** Both columns start at Grade 9 and leave the elementary rows empty rather than inventing coverage — for AP that is a rule, not a choice, since the Course Audit only authorizes courses "offered exclusively in grades 9–12". Their placements refuse to take "college-level" at face value and are weighed against Ontario transfer-credit policy instead, which produced the most uncomfortable finding in the dataset: **Ontario universities do not agree with each other.** Queen's grants AP Calculus BC a full first-year credit (MATH 121, 6.0 units) and Guelph grants MATH 1080, while U of T Arts & Science grants nothing for any AP calculus. Queen's grants nothing for AP English; Guelph grants a full credit; U of T grants half. Offsets follow the weight of that evidence and are held at `approximate`, because a placement that reverses depending on the registrar is not documented. An earlier version of this site derived AP placements from one university's table alone and had AP English a year ahead of AP mathematics; the wider evidence reverses that, and it is the clearest example of why single-source placements are dangerous here.
- **IB is placed as Ontario actually delivers it.** The Primary and Middle Years Programmes are omitted: no TDSB school runs them. What is shown is the real route — two Pre-IB years that "move through the Ontario Curriculum at an accelerated pace", with the TDSB's own examples being that "Math 9 covers some content from the Ontario Grade 10 math curriculum" and that students complete "Grade 11 Ontario courses in the Grade 10 year", followed by the two-year Diploma. The final Diploma year is drawn tall because a student takes three Higher Level subjects, which earn a full first-year credit, alongside three Standard Level subjects, which earn none at any university checked. The cleanest available comparison between the two frameworks is Queen's, where one registrar in one year maps both AP Calculus BC and IB HL Mathematics to MATH 121, both AP French and IB HL French to FREN 150, and grants **neither** AP English nor IB HL English any credit at all. AP and IB are therefore placed level with each other in all three subjects rather than one leading, and English is held back for both.
- **Branksome and UCC accelerate very differently, and both say so themselves.** Branksome's MYP pages publish Ontario course codes grade by grade and footnote which are "Reach Ahead credits": in Grade 8, Math (MTH1W) carries the asterisk and English (ENG8), French and Science (SCI8) do not. Grade 9 is MPM2D in mathematics but ENL1W and SNC1W on grade; Grade 10 is MCR3U against ENG2D and SNC2D. Those English and science zeros are *documented decisions*, not evidence gaps — the school had the option and declined it. UCC instead makes a blanket claim in the document universities read: across Years 8 to 10 "most students are completing courses a year earlier than their age cohort at other Ontario schools", and the same sentence enumerates the subjects it covers, so it can be applied one by one. Its Year 9 Fast Track programme, built for boys arriving on an Ontario Grade 8, is corroboration from the other side — a catch-up track only needs to exist if the cohort is genuinely ahead.
- **Appleby is the clearest case of a directory sentence outrunning the school.** The circulating line "Accelerated French and mathematics is offered in Grades 7-8 and 9-10" is traceable to a republished prospectus whose course tables label four columns "(Accelerated Option Available)". Appleby's own calendar says something narrower and decisive: in Middle Two "Most students will continue to develop their understanding of grade 7 and 8 topics, while some will proceed with additional grade 9 topics." Acceleration is a minority stream, so every Appleby subject sits at the Ontario position with the faster route recorded separately. Its Grade 7 page settles the other half of the claim against it, listing Core and Extended French rather than accelerated French.
- **Crescent's sources contradict each other in exactly one cell.** A directory profile carries Crescent saying its Grades 3–6 mathematics is "one grade-level ahead", while Crescent's own Lower School page has said it "follows the overall and specific expectations of the Ontario Ministry of Education and then enriches each subject level" continuously since at least 2019. The directory claim is discounted to `+0.5` because the same profile also says "Crescent School begins at Grade 3" and describes a streamed Grade 9 mathematics programme, both false today. Note also that Crescent expanded to Grades 1 and 2 in September 2026, so some of its own pages still carry stale "Grades 3–12" strings.
- **SMCS and Lauremont are both placed level, for opposite reasons.** SMCS directs Grades 7–8 families to the Ontario elementary curriculum and offers every core subject at Academic level with no Applied option, which raises the floor without moving the grade; its reach-ahead policy is explicitly framed as breadth, "to broaden the scope of a student's education". Lauremont (formerly Toronto Montessori Schools) states outright that "The Ontario Ministry of Education sets the curriculum and content for each of the courses, while the IB programme both enriches and guides how the content is taught" — a documented zero for Grades 7–12. Its Montessori years are the exception and the site's largest early-years offset: published Children's House outcomes include four-digit multiplication and division by age six, which Ontario places in Grades 4 and 5. That is discounted hard to `+1.5`, because Montessori reaches those operations through concrete materials rather than written fluency, and because they are exit outcomes for a three-year programme rather than a per-year claim.
- **Kumon is the one column whose levels are not years.** They are letters, assigned by placement test rather than by age, so the question its labels answer is narrower: which Kumon level covers the content Ontario covers in that grade. Every offset is therefore zero *by construction* — the label was chosen to match — and Kumon's own claim that students study "two to three grade levels above" is recorded as an available route rather than folded in, because it describes who enrols and stays rather than where the levels sit. Kumon itself says "the Kumon programmes are not designed to follow a school curriculum". Two asymmetries worth knowing: the widely-quoted 7A–O ladder is the union of two programmes, since Math runs 6A–O and Reading runs 7A–L; and Reading stops exactly on Ontario Grade 12 while Math continues three levels past it to differential equations. French and science are `not-offered` — Kumon teaches mathematics and reading only.
- **The York School exposes why structured pathways matter.** Its current guide says Grade 8 students complete MTH1W in the regular program, while its reach-ahead policy still describes admission to that route. The required column therefore stays conservative and a selectable accelerated-mathematics pathway shows the published one-year lead. Full Diploma and flexible IB Courses are separate senior options; their inquiry, external assessment, and course load are recorded primarily as depth rather than automatic timing.
- **Ontario Core, Extended, and Immersion French are separate provincial sequences with an editorial content crosswalk.** Close wording in the official expectations supports Extended at roughly `+1.5` years in Grades 4–8 and `+1` in secondary school, and Immersion at roughly `+3` in Grades 1–3, `+2.5` in Grades 4–8, and `+2` in secondary school. Every non-zero placement is `approximate`: the Ministry publishes no formal pathway equivalencies, and the offsets come from expectation complexity rather than dividing the 600/1,260/3,800 contact-hour requirements. Subjects taught in French retain their normal Ontario expectations, so mathematics and science do not move.
- **Spirit of Math separates early topics from curriculum completion.** Its official scope introduces later-Ontario topics years early and describes intensive, competition-oriented depth, but it also says the program was not designed simply to accelerate through topics. Its timing offsets are approximate ranges summarized as points; none means the student completed every strand of a later Ontario grade.
- **Singapore Math means the national syllabus, not any commercial textbook carrying the name.** Primary 1–4 is common, then Standard/Foundation and secondary G-levels branch by placement. The default Standard/G3 route and the Foundation/G2 route are selectable within one column. Offsets use first required topic appearance and never use international test scores as curriculum evidence.
- **University outcomes are context, not a ranking.** The overlay keeps offers, acceptances, intended destinations, and matriculation distinct; preserves each publisher's original unit; and marks missing cohort denominators. EQAO is omitted because private participation is optional, school-level private files are not public, and Ontario's downloadable school dataset explicitly excludes private schools.
- **Same-position convergence is shown, not smoothed away.** Bayview Glen's Grade 8 and Grade 9 both sit at Ontario Grade 9 in mathematics, because the elementary lead is banked as credits and the Upper School re-anchors to Ontario course codes. The validator rejects only true inversions.
- **"On Ontario timing" is suppressed where Ontario teaches nothing.** A level sitting at zero offset against an empty provincial column is labelled *no Ontario counterpart* instead, so TFS's French-medium Kindergarten is not misread as merely keeping up.
- **French offsets are floors, not measurements.** Ontario's core French ladder (600 hours by Grade 8) cannot express how far ahead a French-medium school is; TFS teaches French "at a mother tongue level" and is capped at `+3` for that reason, and says so in its rationale.
- Kindergarten placements are marked `insufficient-evidence` because no school publishes a Kindergarten comparison against Ontario's.
- **Community and forum evidence on these schools' grade mappings is close to non-existent**, and the data reflects that rather than padding it. Searches across Reddit, RedFlagDeals, College Confidential, and parenting forums produced no thread in which anyone claims a specific grade offset for any of these schools. The finding has held across every round of research, including the searches run for Branksome, UCC, Crescent, St. Michael's, Appleby and Lauremont, so no rule anywhere in the dataset carries `community-reported` confidence. Two attribution traps are worth knowing about: a widely-surfaced PDF labelled "Academic Program" is **Greenwood College School's** handbook, not BSS's, and a "curriculum that runs ahead of the Ontario standard" line belongs to a tutoring company advertising itself. Neither is cited here. Directory sources (Our Kids, Wikipedia) are labelled `secondary-directory` rather than dressed up as primary.

Alignments are editorial aids, not accreditation, placement advice, or formal equivalencies.
